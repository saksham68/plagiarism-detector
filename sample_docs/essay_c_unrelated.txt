Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some
bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. This
process takes place primarily in the chloroplasts of plant cells, where the
pigment chlorophyll absorbs sunlight, mostly in the red and blue wavelength
ranges, while reflecting green light, which is why most plants appear
green to the human eye.

The overall reaction can be summarized as carbon dioxide and water, in the
presence of light energy, being converted into glucose and oxygen. This
oxygen is released into the atmosphere as a byproduct and is essential for
the survival of most aerobic organisms on Earth, including humans.

Photosynthesis is typically divided into two stages: the light-dependent
reactions, which occur in the thylakoid membrane, and the light-independent
reactions, also known as the Calvin cycle, which occur in the stroma of the
chloroplast.
